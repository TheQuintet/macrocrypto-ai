# Product Owner (PO) – Clear Duties & Daily Tasks (3-Month Project)

## Core Responsibilities (High-Level)
- Maximize the value the development team delivers.
- Own, refine, and prioritize the product backlog.
- Ensure clarity on *what* to build and *why*.
- Be the single source of truth for requirements.
- Accept or reject work increments (Definition of Done).
- Manage stakeholders’ expectations and communicate progress.

---

## Daily / Every-Sprint Tasks (Routine)

### Every Day (15–30 min)
1. **Check GitHub Issues & Projects**  
   - Review new comments, questions, or blockers on open issues/user stories.  
   - Respond to clarification requests from the team within 2 hours (max 4).  
   - Ensure sub-issues are correctly linked to parent user stories.

2. **Synchronize with Scrum Master**  
   - Quick 5-min standup follow-up: align on sprint progress, impediments, and any priority shifts.

3. **Review “Ready for PO Review” issues**  
   - If a story is marked as done, validate it against acceptance criteria.  
   - Either accept (close) or reject with clear feedback (re-open with comments).

4. **Update the “Roadmap” Project Board**  
   - Move any completed roadmap themes to “Done”.  
   - Adjust dates if scope or velocity changes.

5. **Refine the “Iterative Development” board**  
   - Ensure the current sprint’s issues are correctly prioritized (top = highest value).  
   - If a new urgent item appears, decide whether to swap it in (and notify the team).

---

### Every Sprint Planning Day (first day of each sprint – 2–3 hours)
- **Before planning:**  
  - Have a refined candidate backlog (top 20–30 issues) with clear acceptance criteria and story points estimated (with team help).  
- **During planning:**  
  - Present the top-priority items for the sprint.  
  - Explain business value and context for each.  
  - Negotiate scope with the team based on capacity (velocity).  
  - Finalize the Sprint Goal and commit to the selected issues.  
- **After planning:**  
  - Update the “Iterative Development” board with the sprint’s issues and set the **Sprint** field on the project board.

---

### Every Sprint Review Day (last day of each sprint – 1 hour)
- **Prepare a demo** (or walkthrough) of accepted stories.  
- **Collect feedback** from stakeholders (could be internal or yourself as proxy).  
- **Update the backlog** based on new insights – add new stories, re-prioritize existing ones.  
- **Adjust the “Roadmap”** if the release date or scope changes.

---

### Every Sprint Retrospective (immediately after review – 45 min)
- Participate actively: share what went well / poorly from a product perspective.  
- Commit to 1–2 action items that improve requirements clarity or stakeholder communication.

---

## Weekly Tasks (every Friday, 1 hour)
1. **Backlog Grooming (Refinement) Session**  
   - With the team (or alone, then sync):  
     - Write/rewrite user stories for the next 2 sprints.  
     - Add acceptance criteria, business rules, and non-functional requirements.  
     - Re-estimate (or ask team to estimate) using story points.  
     - Ensure sub-issues are broken down into tasks no larger than 2 days of work.

2. **Update “Team Planning” Project Board**  
   - Move long-term epics/themes onto the Roadmap view.  
   - Check that dependencies between projects are mapped.

3. **Update “Retrospective” Board**  
   - Add any process-improvement ideas related to product decisions.

4. **Stakeholder Mini-Update**  
   - Send a brief (bullet) summary to all members (since all are owners) about:  
     - What’s done this week.  
     - What’s next.  
     - Any risks or changes in priority.

---

## One-Time / Monthly Tasks (over 3 months)

### Month 1 (Foundation)
- **Define MVP scope** clearly – what is absolutely required for the first release.  
- **Create a release plan** on the “Roadmap” board with three monthly checkpoints.  
- **Establish the Definition of Ready** (DoR) and **Definition of Done** (DoD) with the team.

### Month 2 (Iteration & Adjustment)
- **Review velocity** after Sprint 2 – adjust backlog size for remaining sprints.  
- **Run a mini-stakeholder demo** (internal) to validate direction.

### Month 3 (Finalisation)
- **Focus on hardening** – bugs, documentation, and deployment tasks.  
- **Finalise the release** – ensure all acceptance criteria are met for every story.  
- **Prepare handover documentation** (if needed) – user manuals, known issues.

---

## Decision-Making Rules (for PO alone)
- **Priority = Business Value / Cost** – rank by ROI.  
- **Say “NO”** to scope creep – every new feature must replace an existing one of equal size.  
- **If uncertain**, create a quick spike (small research issue) and assign it to a developer.

---

## Tools & Boards Usage (Github Projects)
| Board | PO’s Responsibility |
|-------|----------------------|
| **Team Planning** | Set high-level epics, release dates, and sprint goals. |
| **Roadmap** | Keep the timeline realistic – update after each sprint. |
| **Iterative Development** | Own the priority order of the current sprint’s issues. |
| **Retrospective** | Add product-related improvement actions (e.g., better acceptance criteria). |

---

## Daily Checklist (printable)
- [ ] Respond to all GitHub notifications (@mentions, comments).  
- [ ] Confirm no story is stuck without PO input.  
- [ ] Move accepted issues to “Done” on the project board.  
- [ ] Ensure the next 3–5 issues in backlog are “Ready” (with criteria).  
- [ ] Sync with Scrum Master on blockers.  
- [ ] Update the “Iterative Development” board priority order if needed.

---

## Success Criteria for PO (by end of 3 months)
- All user stories have clear acceptance criteria and are testable.  
- The team never waits more than 4 hours for a PO decision.  
- The final product meets the MVP goals defined in Month 1.  
- Stakeholders (even if internal) are satisfied with delivered value.
